### 8. p. 342 (sheet 196R, image): Appendix opening, "Decalogue with upper accents": prose only
Verses: Exod 20:2-14; Deut 5:6-18 (cantillation). One page of prose, no table. Content in our words: the Decalogue has two cantillation systems; the lower accents divide it into thirteen verses (matching chap. 5) while the upper accents give one verse per commandment, except that the first two commandments are joined in one verse (taken to mirror the midrashic view that Israel heard those two directly from God). The upper accents are the practice for Shavuot (Ashkenazi) and all public reading (Sephardi); the lower are older.
Feeds: **Side by side** (Ten Words): a small "how the text is divided" note only. Confirm that Tigay's chap. 5 (lower accents) numbering = the standard 5:6-21 verses.

### 13. pp. 62-67 (text): the Decalogue, Exodus vs Deuteronomy (prose, not tabular)
Verses: Deut 5:6-18 (Eng 5:6-21) beside Exod 20:2-17. Tigay lists differences in the comments on each commandment: (a) Sabbath: "observe" in Deut where Exod says "remember"; Deut adds the "as the LORD your God commanded you" clause and "ox, ass, any cattle"; Deut gives a different reason (rest for servants, and the Egypt exodus) where Exod gives the creation rest; (b) the parents command adds "that you may fare well" (not in Exod); (c) the coveting commands: in Exod the neighbour's house is the first term and means household, with wife among its contents; in Deut the wife stands alone in the first clause, the house is a dwelling, field is added, and the second verb differs (longing rather than scheming). He also says the ten are arranged in two groups, duties to God then to people, each in descending gravity, with the parents command as the bridge, and that this edition numbers them as Philo/Josephus do (differing from the synagogue tablets chiefly in splitting the first two).
Feeds: **Side by side** (Ten Words, Deut 5 beside Exod 20): this is the checklist of differences. **lawparallel** only as the "stated differences" source. Unit 07.
Uncertainty: points (a)-(c) are from the Comments (pp. 64-67); the "as the LORD commanded" clause and the exact Exod wording come from the verses, so confirm against both texts. Tigay may list more differences than I read.

### 16. pp. 151-157 (text): the pilgrim festivals of Deut 16:1-17: prose, no table
Verses: 16:1-17 (compare Exod 12-13; 23:14-19; 34:18-26; Lev 23; Num 9; 28-29). Tigay does not tabulate. What he gives for a **calendar** row set (our summary):
- Three festivals only, each at the "chosen place": Pesah with Unleavened Bread (spring; pesah sacrifice at the close of 14th day of month 1, unleavened bread 7 days from the 15th, per Lev 23:6; Num 28:17); Weeks (seven weeks after the harvest begins); Booths (fall, after the ingathering of grain and wine). No dates stated in Deut except "the month of Abib".
- Themes: Exodus memory and harvest thanks. Deut's concern is the one sanctuary (hence only the three pilgrimage feasts). He notes the passages differ in practice and says halakhic and critical readings treat this differently.
Feeds: **calendar** (the Deut 16 column; use Lev 23 and Num 28-29 from other books for the other columns; Tigay supplies the differences only as prose); unit 17. Uncertainty: no table to match; dates for Weeks come from our text, not Tigay.

### 19. Deut 28 structure (Excursus 26, pp. 489-492): blessings and curses
Verses: 28:1-68. Tigay's divisions: A blessings (1-14); B curses (15-68) in three parts, B1 15-44, B2 45-57, B3 58-68. The six explicit blessings (3-6) are matched, nearly word for word, by the six explicit curses (16-19). Then two chiastic lists:
- Promises (7-13a), each answering a blessing in reverse order: economic success (3a), soil fertility (3b), fertility of people and herds (4), food (5), military success (6); then military (7), food (8), fertility (11), soil (12a), economic success (12b-13).
- Threats (20-44): intro (20), then the standard triad (disease, drought/famine, defeat; 22-26) and the chiasm: A farm disaster (23-24); B defeat/byword (25-26); C inflammation (27); D madness, blindness (28-29a); E robbery (29b); F oppression (30-33a); E' robbery (33b); D' madness from what one sees (34); C' inflammation (35); B' exile/byword (36-37); A' farm disaster (38-42); then loss of economic standing (43-44) reversing the promise of 12b-13.
- B2 (45-57) and B3 (58-68) run in cause-and-effect order (invasion, siege, cannibalism; disease, exile, Egypt); v. 45 is a hinge that would also close B1.
Feeds: **treaty overlay** (the sanctions section's internal map); **mirror seed** (Lev 26 with Deut 28: Tigay calls both the "tokhehah"; cross-refs: Lev 26:19 with Deut 28:23, and Lev 26:26 with the Aramaic curse Tigay cites; Lev 26 itself is only touched in passing); unit 30 (peak).

### 24. pp. 446-449 (text): Excursus 13: the arrangement of the laws (12:2-26:15)
The fullest structured outline in the book. Five sections:
- **I. The sanctuary and other religious matters (12:2-16:17)**
 - A. Place of worship (12:2-13:1 Heb = Eng 12:2-32): destroy Canaanite sanctuaries (2-3); restrict sacrifice to one place (4-7); explication (8-28); shun Canaanite practices (29-13:1).
 - B. Instigation to worship other gods (13:2-19 Heb = Eng 13:1-18): prophet or dreamer (2-6); relative or friend (7-12); a whole town (13-19).
 - C. Holiness (14:1-21): mourning (1-2); diet (3-21).
 - D. Periodic duties (14:22-16:17): tithes (annual 22-27, triennial 28-29); the poor (15:1-18: remission of debts 1-6, lending 7-11, release of servants 12-18); firstlings (15:19-23); pilgrimage festivals (16:1-17).
- **II. Civil and religious authorities (16:18-18:22)**: A judiciary (16:18-17:13: judges 16:18-20; three worship prohibitions 16:21-17:1; prosecuting apostates 17:2-7; high court 17:8-13); B king (17:14-20); C Levites' dues (18:1-8); D prophet (18:9-22).
- **III. Judicial and military matters (19:1-21:9)**: A courts (19: asylum cities 1-13; boundary markers 14; witnesses 15-21); B warfare (20: army 1-9; defeated cities 10-18; trees 19-20); C unsolved murder (21:1-9).
- **IV. Miscellaneous laws, mostly civil and domestic (21:10-25:19)**: A family (21:10-21: captive wife 10-14; firstborn 15-17; rebellious son 18-21); B body of an executed criminal (21:22-23); C miscellaneous domestic (22:1-12: lost animals 1-3; fallen animals 4; clothing of the opposite sex 5; mother bird 6-7; parapet 8; mixed seed 9; mixed plowing 10; mixed fibres 11; tassels 12); D marital and sexual misconduct (22:13-29); E forbidden relationships (Heb 23:1-9; father's former wife; entry to the assembly 2-9); F miscellaneous (Heb 23:10-25:19: camp sanctity; fugitive slaves; prostitution; interest; vows; neighbour's crops; remarriage 24:1-4; newlywed 5; millstone 6; kidnapping 7; skin disease 8-9; pledges 10-13; wages 14-15; individual guilt 16; aliens and widows 17-18; gleanings 19-22; flogging 25:1-3; threshing ox 4; levirate 5-10; intervening in a fight 11-12; weights 13-16; Amalek 17-19).
- **V. Liturgical declarations (26:1-15)**: first fruits (1-11); tithe declaration (12-15).
His principle: linking by "concatenation" (ideas, key words, motifs) rather than topic; he gives detailed reasons per junction (pp. 449-458), e.g. the three-item "build a house, plant a vineyard, marry" order matching 20:5-8; 19:14's boundary markers sandwiched by murder and perjury, matching the Decalogue order; the pairing of war and trials.
Feeds: **unit map** (check boundaries of units 13-28; see end), **lawparallel** (the junction reasons for why each Deut law sits where it does).

### 25. Excursus 16 (pp. 466-469, text): Deut 15 vs Exod 21-23 and Lev 25: a comparison without a table
Verses: 15:1-18; Exod 21:2-11; 23:10-11; Lev 25. Tigay's points, in our terms:
- Deut and Exod share: the slave called "Hebrew"; six-year service; release in the seventh; the ritual of the awl for the one who stays. Exod's fallowing of the land and release of a daughter sold for marriage are not repeated in Deut but not contradicted. Debt remission in the seventh year is probably new in Deut.
- Lev 25 is incompatible in detail: manumission at the jubilee, not after six years; service can run up to fifty years; terms differ (land "sabbath" vs "dropping/releasing"); a system designed for families and clans, where Exod/Deut protect individuals. Not derived from Exod 21-23.
- Also: two "seventh years" in Deut 15 (a shared year of remission in v. 1, the servant's own seventh year in v. 12); Jer 34 treats them as one. The prosbul of Hillel (end of first century BCE) circumvents remission.
Feeds: **lawparallel** (the best Exod/Lev/Deut triple: servant release, with explicit stated differences); **mirror seed** (Lev 25 / Deut 15); units 16.

### 26. Excursus 2 (pp. 422-430, text): Deut 1-3 against the other accounts
Verses: 1:7; 1:9-18; 1:19-46; 1:37; 1:46-2:8; 2:1-3:11 (with Num 13-14, 20-21, 33; Exod 18).
Contrasts Tigay draws (these are the "stated differences" for Side by side):
- **Northern border**: Euphrates (Deut 1:7; 11:24) vs Lebo-hamath (Num 13:21; 34:8). He ties each to a pre-Israelite view of Canaan's extent.
- **Chiefs (1:9-15 vs Exod 18:13-26)**: Moses proposes and the people nominate (Deut); Jethro proposes and Moses chooses (Exod); intellectual vs ethical qualifications.
- **Scouts (1:22-25, 36 vs Num 13-14)**: the people ask to send scouts for military and route intelligence (Deut) vs God commands it (Num); Caleb alone is exempted in Deut; Joshua is not named.
- **Punishment of Moses (1:37)**: blamed on the people's sin vs his own sin at Meribah.
- **Route and chronology (1:46-2:14)**: Kadesh in the second year then the Gulf of Elath and the east of Edom (Deut) vs a Kadesh visit in the fortieth year and Mt Hor and a northern crossing (Num 20; 33). Four discrepancies are listed: (1) two Kadesh visits; (2) whether Israel reached the Gulf; (3) Beeroth-bene-jaakan/Moserah vs Moseroth/Bene-jaakan and where Aaron died (Mt Hor); (4) Edom refused passage (Num 20:14-21) vs allowed it (Deut 2:4-8, 29). Harmonistic vs source-critical solutions are set out.
Feeds: **Side by side** (the Deut 1-3 seeds), **maps** (Map 3's two-route overlay); units 01-03.

### 27. Excursus 12 (p. 445, text): the golden calf, Exod 32 and Deut 9:9-21
Deut's account is shorter and omits Aaron's making of the calf and several Exod details (the unit map lists it as unit 11). Tigay's discussion covers how Deut treats the calf as an offence against the first commandment and why the Aaron story may have been softened in Exod. Not read in detail.
Feeds: **Side by side** (golden calf). Unit 11. Uncertainty: no structured comparison found; read the Excursus before building.

### 28. Excursus 1 (pp. 417-421, text): historical geography gazetteer
