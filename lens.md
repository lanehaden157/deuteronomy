# Deuteronomy lens

How to look at the passage. Every card says to read this first.

Intertextuality first. What earlier Scripture stands behind a line, where line or word returns later in the canon (New Testament included), what the echo does. Then narrative structure, keywords, type-scenes, the creation–covenant–exile–presence story. No symmetry the text doesn't show. Plausible but thin link → bring to Lane marked tentative, don't drop. Medieval commentators and fine grammar = one voice among several.

## This book

**TENTATIVE draft, unchecked against the commentaries.**

Moses' speeches on the plains of Moab, in the fortieth year, to the generation about to cross. Four movements: retelling the past, the law and the covenant, song and blessing, Moses' death. The speaker is Moses, addressing y'all directly; "today" and "you" are the book's own pressure points.

Look first for:
- **Retelling.** Where Moses recasts Exodus–Numbers (Horeb, Kadesh, Sihon and Og, the calf), what he trims, adds or reassigns. Compare the earlier account, don't merge them. The box for the two accounts is labelled **Side by side**.
- **Law restated.** Where a statute parallels Exodus or Leviticus, what differs and the reason Moses gives. Differences and stated reasons only; dating debates go in a footnote.
- **Covenant shape.** Loyalty, remember/forget, hear/do, love, the one place, blessing and curse, witnesses. Treaty parallels are a later overlay: don't lean on them yet.
- **Return of keywords.** Hear (shama), today, remember, inherit, the place Yahweh will choose, "that it may go well with you".
- **Poetry and blessing.** The Song and the Blessing use the canon's poetry handling: parallelism, imagery, tribe-by-tribe sayings (Gen 49 beside ch. 33).
- **Ending.** Moses dies outside the land; Joshua, the unrepeated prophet, and the Torah as book.

Genre cautions:
- Speech, not narrator: attribute claims to Moses, not to Yahweh or the narrator, unless the text does.
- Singular/plural "you" shifts (Numeruswechsel) may carry meaning or be style: note, don't decide.
- Warnings and curses are conditional; don't read them as outcomes before the text does.
- Devotion to destruction (herem) texts: report what is said and for whom; no softening, no gloss.
- Hebrew/English verse numbers differ in 74 verses; cite English.
